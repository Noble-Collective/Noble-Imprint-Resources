# Further Resources

## Study Design

### Seeing the Design

Engineering a pedagogically sound modern-day catechism rooted in Scripture in a way that serves disciples and churches on mission is no small feat. While we desire to honor the saints of old, we unashamedly root this work in inspired Scripture. Hoping to build strong church communities, we also want the advance of the gospel. We desire deep reflection on divine truth at the same time that we aim for heart impact, life change, and inner transformation. Simultaneously, we want this study to be accessible to new believers and helpful for lay leaders. In this way, the curricular shape of this series has an intentional design in order to be biblically oriented, theologically robust, spiritually formative, community-based, and missionally practical. The following details help to explain the design "under-the-hood" of this series.

### Five Movements: The Formative Structure of the Main Sessions

The structure of each main session has been designed with Christian formation and the Great Commission in mind. The five movements in each session teach disciples to collectively engage the biblical text in a transformative way as they serve God and his work in the world. With this formative design consistently applied to each session, such a hermeneutical (or interpretive) process serves to equip the church with the ability to grow as skillful readers of Scripture beyond the scope of this series. As each session unfolds, its purposeful design serves the maturity of the church through a process that is sound in its pedagogy, collaborative in its experience, distinctively Christian in its character, and missional in orientation. Workable in either an oral or literate context (or some blend of the two), this process aims to situate transformative Bible reading in the context of the church. Toward this end, the session structure integrates scriptural exegesis (or study), theological discussion, personal application, ministry training, and missional engagement. It is ordered in a way that cultivates meaningful reflection on the Bible, God, self, church, and world. In what follows, we briefly survey this fivefold framework of formative Scripture reading.

#### Biblical Interpretation: Hearing the Word

God's revealed word to his people is the only reliable and sure guide for the Christian life. Our maturity in the Christian faith is nourished by faithful study of the Scriptures. In order to understand the truth of God's word, we need to apprehend the various components of the biblical text (i.e., structure, theme, plot, events, characters, logical flow, sequence). Therefore, after the session **Introduction**, the first section of each session is called **Biblical Interpretation: Hearing the Word**. This section aims to orient the community of interpreters to the major details of the selected biblical story. Through purposeful reading practices, focused observation questions, general textual exploration, and a summative storycrafting review, this section familiarizes disciples with the biblical text that is being studied. A visual of "Narrative Structure" helps to map the typical features and flow of good stories. The goal of this section is to help readers recall basic textual details, trace the overall narrative flow, and discern the structural design of each biblical narrative. Accurately grasping these details will provide a fitting foundation to explore the significance of the passage at a theological level.

#### Theological Dialogue: Discussing the Plot

As God's divinely inspired word, Scripture teaches us how to rightly understand reality. For each session of the _Narrative Journey_, a narrative (or narrative-like) portion of Scripture has been carefully selected based on the way the passage addresses a particular aspect of the Christian faith. While there are many viable themes addressed by any given biblical text, each session focuses its attention on a particular topic germane to the selected passage of Scripture. In this regard, the second movement of each session is called **Theological Dialogue: Discussing the Plot**. This movement helps the Christian community explore the overall storyline of the biblical narrative at a theological level, allowing the unfolding plot of each narrative to uniquely inform a distinctively Christian approach to the life of faith. The "Narrative Theology" infographic provides insight into the formative impact of stories. Through discussion questions, narrative reflection, and textual commentary, Christian disciples search the scriptural text to know God more truly and understand reality more accurately by coming to terms with the theological agenda of the specific passage being studied. Grounded in the theological significance of the passage, Christian readers should learn how to apply the passage to their own lives.

#### Personal Reflection: Entering the Story

As much as God's word offers a framework of Christian belief, it also outlines a pathway for godly living. Our understanding of the Scriptures deepens as we put the truth of God's word to practice in our own lives. Without allowing God's word to deeply penetrate our lives, we will remain at a superficial understanding of Scripture and a theoretical understanding of godliness. God gave us the Bible as a means to diagnose our spiritual condition and prescribe the life of faith. As a mirror, the Bible examines our life and instructs us on how to better live as disciples of Christ. Every biblical text is a summons to discipleship. Accordingly, the third movement of each session is called **Personal Reflection: Entering the Story**. At this point in the session flow, disciples allow the biblical passage to evaluate and instruct their personal lives and then discuss it together in community. Through reflection questions and spiritual exercises, this movement encourages members of the Christian community to examine their lives in the mirror of the biblical text and to build new faith habits by putting scriptural truth into practice. The "Spiritual Practices" figure summarizes twelve key Christian disciplines that should be developed in a believer's life. Taking time to personally reflect on the passage and apply its teachings to life will situate disciples to be able to minister this passage to others.

#### Ministry Practice: Rehearsing the Script

God's word is a life-giving instrument of divine revelation. Inasmuch as the Bible impacts our own Christian formation, the Scriptures should also serve the maturity of the whole church. To the extent that we earnestly apply the truth to our own lives, we will be better equipped to minister the truth to others in a way that edifies the community of faith. Toward this end, the fourth movement to each session, **Ministry Practice: Rehearsing the Script**, allocates space for disciples to use each selected passage of Scripture in some form of ministry. In an effort to make this series explicitly church-based, we designed a process that puts the community of faith at the center of its catechetical process. Through practical reflection, suggested ministry ideas, collaborative design, and strategic implementation, disciples consider how each passage serves the church. In order to accommodate varying levels of Christian maturity and differing roles in the church community, disciples should think about how this passage can be used in a family context, mentoring relationship, home gathering, church meeting, network structure, or global setting. The "Ministry Practices" visual outlines some of these ministry ideas. As the church is built up, God's people become equipped to bear witness of God's truth to the wider world.

#### Missional Outreach: Publicizing the Truth

As they accept God's invitation to global mission, Christians have been called to witness the truth of God's word to the unbelieving world. Not only does sharing our faith deepen our understanding of God's word, it also provides a fitting context for faith formation. One of the main outlets (and aims) for Christian maturity is public witness. The closing focus of each session is **Missional Outreach: Publicizing the Truth**. In this portion of each study, the community of disciples goes out into the world to share the truth of God's word with the lost. The goal of this final movement is to deploy the church in her missional vocation. Through contextual reflection, suggested outreach ideas, collaborative vision, and practical implementation, disciples publicize the truth of God's word by voicing and expressing it to the surrounding world. While each passage may not explicitly speak about Christ, each biblical narrative is a part of a larger Scripture (meta)narrative that tells God's unfolding redemptive plan. In this way, every passage of the Bible can be preached to unbelievers, inviting them to faith in Christ. The final infographic, "Mission Practices," offers some ideas to speak and embody the truth to the surrounding world. As a culmination in this formative engagement of Scripture, the believing community becomes a witness of God's truth, cementing its understanding of the biblical text by becoming an advocate for its truthfulness in the wider world.

#### Summary

In sum, the common framework overlaid in each session attempts to outline a _formative hermeneutic_: a transformative way to engage God's word as a community of faith. Through this study process—an intentionally designed community exploration—God's people gather around carefully selected biblical passages as a means to become established in the faith. In this community study process, disciples engage the text, discuss its truth, examine their lives, edify the faith community, and bear witness to the truth in their spheres of influence. Engaging the text in this way not only anchors the church in the truth of God's word, it also teaches disciples how to study any passage of Scripture in a well-rounded and formative manner. Moreover, by placing the Scriptures in the center of the church, the community of faith grows to maturity and becomes equipped as a witness to the surrounding world.

### Five Reflections: The Pastoral Design of the Opening and Recall

Each study has an introductory and concluding session. The introductory session ("The Opening") helps to orient disciples and churches to the study. The concluding session ("The Recall") encourages a celebratory review. In order to assist in the work of orientation and celebration, these sessions follow a unique fivefold movement. Consider below how these sessions are intentionally designed to serve disciples and churches in this spiritually formative journey.

These sessions begin with **Scripture Meditation: Surveying the Landscape**. In this opening movement, disciples reflect on a brief psalm to prime their minds and hearts to the subject of study. Second, **Faith Foundation: Exploring the Terrain** facilitates contemplative discussion around a historic quote and key ideas related to the study's main topic. The third movement, **Learning Plan: Charting the Course**, sketches the content of the book along with some personal reflection for the spiritual journey ahead. Fourth, the **Doctrinal Summary: Synthesizing the Faith** introduces key faith summaries and a personal project to be completed over the course of the study. Finally, **Faith Practice: Following the Way** commits the time of study to the Lord, seeking his unmerited grace and transformative power in our lives and communities. These five reflections are reused in the final session of this book to review the study, reflect on key learning, internalize biblical principles, crystallize one's own thinking, and entrust oneself (and others) to the Lord.

Although these studies can be completed individually, there is great power in making this journey as a faith community. Resist the temptation to rush into the first main session of study. Instead, patiently set the context of this study by organizing the whole community in a way that could actually bear greater fruit than a mere individual venture. From a heart of pastoral concern and with a display of shepherding wisdom, use the introductory and concluding sessions to not just get everyone in your community "on the same page," but moving "in the same direction"—on a biblically based journey during which they can encounter God's transformative presence.

### The Christian Commons: A Note on Copyright and Licensing

A core objective of this series is to make available to the global church a robust and reproducible process of establishing believers in the faith. To facilitate this vision, we have taken a strategic stance on copyright and licensing. As part of a larger effort to provide unrestricted resources for the global church, we are publishing the content of the _Narrative Journey_ series under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike license (CC BY-SA), version 4.0. A large amount of quality Christian content and training resources already exist in the world. However, many of these resources are "locked down" under traditional, antiquated copyright restrictions. Instead, a CC BY-SA 4.0 license releases this content to the global church; it enables church leaders to provide sound theological content and formative resources to the church at large to use in their own contexts. The nature of this license allows us to "lock open" this content for Christians throughout the world. As seen in the very design of this series, this licensing decision is motivated by the conviction that the essentials of the faith—the core teachings of Christianity—duly belong to the church.

### Conclusion

All curricular designs are human inventions. They are not perfect. Only God's word is inspired and inerrant. But for all their flaws, consistent processes of study can be extremely useful in faith formation. Such processes are ways of having a teacher's assistance without a teacher's actual presence. Receive this study's design as a humble attempt to guide disciples and churches in sound biblical interpretation toward life formation and mission. Remember the main aim of this study is to help people engage God's word. Armed with a biblical text, good questions, and some faith practices, a lot of progress can be made in the life of a disciple or church. Condense, adjust, adapt, or rearrange it as needed, keeping the biblical text and its main teachings central. But however you approach discipling believers, let your focus remain unflinchingly on God's word—"a lamp to my feet and a light to my path" (Psalm 119:105).

## Bibliography

### Select References, Reading, and Resources

Although it seeks to be contemporary in its design and shape, this resource is not innovative in its core ideas. It finds itself in a long tradition of faithful biblical scholarship and catechetical instruction. This series was written (and rewritten) over years of study, preaching, teaching, and development in the context of church community and a shared leadership ministry. For the usability of this resource as an establishing tool for Christians and churches, we have opted not to include footnotes in the sessions themselves. When Scripture is quoted (throughout the book) or a historic quote is cited (mainly in introductory epigraphs), source material is referenced. In lieu of footnotes, we have given reference to resources germane to the biblical passages and theological topics.

In what follows, there is a list of resources that have been helpful in this study—resources that have offered specific details, general insights, and noteworthy observations on the passage or topic. This list also includes resources for further study. These resources include commentaries that address the particular biblical passage being studied along with other written works that address the specific topic of the session. Many are simply resources for continued reflection on these matters. This list is not exhaustive. While not every facet of each resource is recommended, in its own way, each resource contributes to the overall conversation of understanding the essentials of the Christian faith. In this way, the following resource summary represents a blend of aids used in the creation of this tool and a bank of resources for ongoing spiritual growth.
