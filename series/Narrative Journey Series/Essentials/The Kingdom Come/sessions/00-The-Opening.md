## Introduction

> Of this you have heard before in the word of the truth, the gospel, which has come to you, as indeed in the whole world it is bearing fruit and increasing—as it also does among you, since the day you heard it and understood the grace of God in truth.

<< Colossians 1:5-6

> The chief end, for which the Church ought to exist—the chief end, for which individual church-members ought to live, is the evangelization or conversion of the world.

<< Alexander Duff, _Missions the Chief End of the Christian Church_

God calls us to testify to others that our lives have been transformed by Christ! _Evangelism_ is the natural outflow of _conversion_. For believers in Jesus, we have experienced the overwhelming love of God in the forgiveness of our sin. In evangelism, we want to tell the whole world how they too can experience this freedom from sin and condemnation. Through faith in Christ, disciples receive the promise of eternal life. Secure in an everlasting future, disciples want more people to enter into this inheritance of unending life. When believers surrender their lives to Jesus, they become citizens of God's righteous kingdom. Moved by their own satisfaction in Christ, Christians announce the kingdom of God to all the residents of earth. Feasting on the promises of God—promises of enduring peace, secure protection, deep joy, and lasting fulfillment—Christians bear witness to others, telling them the source of these life blessings.

In this way, communicating the gospel with others is more than sharing about a particular ideology, offering a religious opinion, or persuading people to join a social club of shared interests. While the Christian faith rests on certain ideas, involves personal faith, and offers community life, Christians are not unified by these commonalities alone. It is the living and risen Jesus, the Savior of the world, around whom all believers gather and testify. At its core, evangelism means to bear witness to the good news of salvation in Jesus. Christian witness is an announcement to the world that _the kingdom has come because the King has come_. Jesus came to earth to remove people from the sinful and destructive kingdom of darkness and invite them into the just and life-giving kingdom of light.

This call to tell others about Jesus is woven into the very fabric of a disciple's life. It is not an optional work. All believers are called to testify about Jesus to the surrounding world. In fact, bearing witness to Jesus gives evidence that our faith is genuine. God does not ask us to testify about something that we have not personally experienced. In its simplest form, evangelism involves sharing with others about how Jesus forgives sin, removes shame, absolves guilt, and grants eternal life. Christians can speak authoritatively (and thus boldly) about these truths because they have experienced these glorious realities in their own life.

When Christians talk to others about Jesus, they are telling people about who Jesus is and what he has done. The gospel is _historical_ in that Jesus came to earth, ministered to people, suffered at the hands of sinful people, died on the cross, and rose again from the dead. This highlights _the work Jesus has done_: accomplishing salvation by become a substitute and sacrifice for sin. The gospel is also _personal_, highlighting the work Jesus does in our lives. Because Jesus is alive today, the work he accomplished historically on the cross can bring transformation in the lives of people today when people place their faith and trust in him. This emphasizes _the work Jesus does_: saving people from their sin when they entrust their lives to Jesus as Savior and Lord. The good news of the gospel is _hopeful_. While we enjoy God's saving work in our present lives, we await a future in which God will complete his work of redemption and remove all that is broken and painful in this world. This speaks about _the work Jesus will do_: one day Jesus will return to earth and bring full and final restoration to his people.

Even though evangelism is a core part of our Christian life, many believers struggle to participate in the work. Fear of rejection and surrender to our insecurities can cause people to be reserved about sharing their faith with others. The verbal nature of evangelism causes apprehension among people who feel ill-equipped at speaking. Many believers live in a context that is hostile to the Christian faith. To invite others to follow Jesus means their own lives are at risk. Still others are simply unaware or uninformed about the work. Part of Christian maturity involves becoming an active and engaged witness for Christ. In fact, a disciple cannot grow to full maturity if they do not participate in this work. In an effort to build solid foundations in the faith and lead others to Christ, this study outlines biblical teaching on evangelism.

In this study, we will turn to twelve key biblical narratives to instruct, train, and mobilize us to Christian witness. The first and final narratives focus on two Old Testament leaders: Jonah and Moses. Through their ministry callings and personal reservations, we come to understand God's heart for mission and we honestly assess own hesitations. Additionally, bracketing the whole study with these two narratives from the Old Testament shows that the New Testament call to Christian evangelism has continuity with God's great work of witness throughout the ages. After the Jonah narrative, we will study six biblical passages from the Gospels. This will be followed by four key narratives from the book of Acts. Dividing the narratives in this way allows us to learn from (and see the continuity between) the ministry of Jesus and the apostles.

The twelve selected stories serve as a firm foundation for understanding and embracing Christian evangelism. While these are not the only biblical passages about this important topic, they do serve as a helpful orientation.The narrative of Jonah 1:1-4:11, teaches us that God is a missionary God with a heart for the whole world. The ministry of John the Baptist recorded in John 1:1-51 instructs us about the basic work and core motivations of Christian witness. When Jesus called his first disciples in Luke 5:1-32, we learn how mission is a fundamental part of Christian discipleship. The sending of the twelve apostles in Matthew 9:35-11:1 clarifies the nature and experience our mission assignment. Jesus told three parables about seeds in Mark 4:1-34 which help us understand how people come to genuine faith in Christ and how the kingdom grows across human history. The threefold set of parables in Luke 15:1-32 emphasize the responsibility to seek after unbelievers like those who are lost.

The parable of the Samaritan in Luke 10:25-37 highlights the role of love, compassion, and good works in Christian evangelism. Acts 3:1-4:31 records the bold and courageous ministry of Peter and John in Jerusalem, teaching us about miracles, the gospel message, and prayer. The important narrative in Acts 6:1-8:4 shows the collective witness of the Christian church as they met the needs of community members. It also chronicles the ministry and martyrdom of Stephen, alerting us to the sacrifice and persecution disciples face as they bear witness to Jesus. Paul's ministry in Athens is recorded in Acts 17:16-34. This biblical story focuses on how Christians can engage with people from other worldview perspectives. Specifically, it teaches us about gospel preaching, preevangelism, contextualization, and anticipated responses to Christian witness. As an example of apologetics (defending the rationality of the faith), Paul's speech before Agrippa in Acts 26:1-32 gives us insight into how we can show the credibility of the Christian faith. Last, we read the call of Moses recorded in Exodus 3:1-4:17. As we study about Moses' reservations to accept his call to ministry, we can evaluate our own hesitations about evangelism and seek God's strength to fully engage the work with faith and courage.

God has placed you in a specific sphere of influence. While some may travel far and wide in efforts to spread the gospel, others may remain in their local areas. Regardless of the scope of one's ministry sphere, the basic duty of Christian witness is a responsibility that all Christians bear. While this study will seek to help you understanding the basics of evangelism, it will also assist you in exploring the specific ways God wants you to bear witness in your spheres of influence. Ultimately, God's call on your life to follow Jesus is interwoven with God's call on your life to represent Jesus to others.

Like any process of maturity, exploring the work of evangelism is not easy. This exploration will cause us to evaluate our hearts, appraise our thinking, and assess our devotion about the Christian faith. For those active in the evangelistic work, this study can help sure up the foundations in your ongoing witness. For others who are essentially uninvolved in evangelism, these twelve passages of Scripture can help mobilize you to the work. More than a burdensome duty or a joyless obligation, the work of evangelism can be (and is) a work of deep delight. For what greater privilege and what better honor is there than to represent our King and Savior to the world. Equipped by the strength of God's power, readied by memory of our faith testimony, and united together with believers across the generations and the globe, we have been given the right and responsibility to announce to the world that the kingdom has come. Our invitation to the world is to submit to King Jesus in faith and find a glorious entrance and inheritance as citizens of the kingdom of God. In this narrative journey of Christian witness, may you, your family, and your church see and embrace this missional vision of a disciple's life.

| **Session 1: The Mission**<br>(Jonah 1:1–4:11) | The prophet Jonah reluctantly accepts his missionary call and learns about God's missionary heart. |
| **Session 2: The Work**<br>(John 1:1–51) | John the Baptist and other early disciples tell others about Jesus. |
| **Session 3: The Call**<br>(Luke 5:1–32) | Jesus calls his first followers to a life of discipleship and mission. |

| **Session 5: The Soils**<br>(Mark 4:1–34) | Jesus tells parables that teach about genuine faith and the growth of God's kingdom. |
| **Session 6: The Lost**<br>(Luke 15:1–32) | Jesus emphasizes the importance of seeking after the lost. |
| **Session 7: The Samaritan**<br>(Luke 10:25–37) | Jesus teaches about the importance of deeds of mercy through the example of the Good Samaritan. |
| **Session 8: The Encounter**<br>(Acts 3:1–4:31) | Peter and John heal a beggar and preach the gospel with boldness. |
| **Session 9: The Stones**<br>(Acts 6:1–8:4) | The early church and Stephen model the practice of good works and sacrificial witness in the face of persecution. |
| **Session 10: The Forum**<br>(Acts 17:16–34) | Paul addresses pressing worldview concerns to his audience in Athens. |
