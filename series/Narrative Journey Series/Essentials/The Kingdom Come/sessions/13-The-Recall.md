## Conclusion

> For what we proclaim is not ourselves, but Jesus Christ as Lord, with ourselves as your servants for Jesus' sake.

<< 2 Corinthians 4:5

> We who formerly delighted in fornication, but now embrace chastity alone; we who formerly used magical arts, dedicate ourselves to the good and unbegotten God; we who valued above all things the acquisition of wealth and possessions, now bring what we have into a common stock, and communicate to every one in need; we who hated and destroyed one another, and on account of their different manners would not live with men of a different tribe, now, since the coming of Christ, live familiarly with them, and pray for our enemies, and endeavour to persuade those who hate us unjustly to live comformably to the good precepts of Christ, to the end that they may become partakers with us of the same joyful hope of a reward from God the ruler of all.

<< Justin Martyr, _The First Apology_

The Christian faith is a spiritual adventure, an exciting and faith-filled journey of following Christ. Fundamental to the gospel message, disciples are born (really reborn) into a new life when they place their trust in Jesus. Upon a person's genuine repentance, surrender, and submission to Christ, believers in Jesus receive unparalleled blessings: forgiveness of sin, exoneration from guilt, removal of shame, the gift of community, and the promise of eternal life. According to God's design, the glorious blessings of the Christian life move and motivate disciples to broadcast the gospel message to the surrounding world. The joy of the Christian life is the determined hope and sheer delight that others will share in your joy by placing their faith in Christ. In sum, evangelistic work is the effervescent overflow of Christ-centered joy.

Joy and delight are not often emotions associate with evangelism. For many, the thought of sharing one's faith with others (let alone just speaking to them) evokes fear, anxiety, and unease. Perhaps this comes from common misrepresentations of evangelism which uphold charismatic preachers as exemplars of the work. Reservations around evangelism may also be traced to misunderstandings. Even more, the blessings of salvation in Christ can create a comfortable and cozy life in which people want to minimize risks and bask in God's provisions for their life. Whatever the reasons, Christian engagement in evangelism often does not correspond to the actual experience of divine favor on one's life. This study seeks to rectify this imbalance and restore the joy of Christian witness.

The twelve carefully selected biblical passages in this study provide a comprehensive and engaging look at the biblical teaching of evangelism. In contrast to the reluctance and reservation of God's prophet, Jonah 1:1-4:11 also highlights God's sincere and compassionate heart to reach the world. The biblical record of John 1:1-51 focuses on the ministry of John the Baptist, a sterling example of Christian witness. The calling of the first disciples in Luke 5:1-32 displays the fusion of discipleship and mission. The commission of the twelve apostles in Matthew 9:35-11:1 gives a broad outline to the realities of Christian mission. Mark 4:1-34 provides insight into the nature of conversion and kingdom growth. Luke 10:25-37 raises the theme of good works and Luke 15:1-32 emphasizes the importance of seeking after the lost.

In Acts 3:1-4:31, the ministry of Peter and John show the bold witness of the early Christians, a model commended to us. Acts 6:1-8:4 teaches us how the gospel can spread in the face of fierce persecution. Paul's work in Athens is recorded in Acts 17:16-34. This biblical narrative helps us learn about the work of preevangelism: removing worldview barriers to the gospel. Paul's skillful speech in Acts 26:1-32 instructs us about the basic aims of apologetic witness: offering a clear and credible defense to the Christian faith. The final narrative is Exodus 3:1-4:17 in which we learn about Moses' call to ministry, Moses' reservations to accept this call, and God's patient and firm response to these reservations.

While the Bible has much more that can be said about Christian witness, this study serves as an orientation to this very important teaching. As important as it is for Christians to grow in right belief, godly conduct, spiritual habits, ordered relationships, and faith devotion, it is also vital that Christians learn how to share their faith with others. In fact, our participation in this evangelistic task is not only part of God's plan to reach the nations, it is also a crucial step in our own spiritual growth. As God has designed it, our compassionate appeal for the surrounding world to believe in Christ is a platform upon which our faith is tested, matured, and strengthened.
